## Unit overview

A 5-day pattern unit framed as a Kindergarten parade. Children join Coco the Conductor and the Parade Animals to spot patterns, find the beat of the parade (the core), translate that beat into different forms, fix broken parade lines, and finally plan and lead their own pattern parade. Designed for play-based, oral, and movement-rich Kindergarten classrooms.

**Unit learning goal:** Children will identify, describe, extend, and create repeating patterns, recognize the core of a pattern, and represent the same core in different ways.

### Curriculum expectations

- **A7.1** — identify, describe, extend, and create patterns
- **A7.2** — identify the core of a repeating pattern and describe why it is important
- **A7.3** — show that patterns can be represented in different ways because the core remains the same
- **A7.4** — extend patterns in both directions and identify any missing elements

### Recurring characters

- **Coco the Conductor** — The parrot leader who keeps the parade in time. Appears on every lesson cover and every worksheet header.
  *Visual:* Friendly cartoon parrot, red and yellow feathers, holding a small wooden conductor's baton. Big eyes, warm smile. Kindergarten-appropriate, soft outlines, no scary features.
- **The Parade Animals** — A small Canadian-animal cast that forms the parade. Used to build, fix, and extend pattern lines across all 5 lessons.
  *Visual:* Four characters drawn in matching cartoon style: Bea the Bear (brown), Finn the Fox (orange), Bibi the Beaver (dark brown), and Moss the Moose (tan). Same outline weight, same eye style, same friendly expression. Each animal always wears a small coloured vest (Bea = red, Finn = blue, Bibi = green, Moss = yellow) so colour and animal attributes can be used together or apart.

### Threaded vocabulary

- **pattern** — Something that repeats in the same order.
- **core** — The little part of the pattern that keeps repeating.
- **repeat** — To do or show something again, the same way.
- **extend** — To keep the pattern going.
- **missing** — A part of the pattern that is not there yet.
- **attribute** — Something we can notice about an item, like its colour, size, or shape.

### Assessment strategy

- **Diagnostic:** Lesson 1 circle-time observation: which children can already say what comes next in a 2-element repeating pattern. Recorded on a class checklist.
- **Formative:** Lesson 2 (core identification) and Lesson 4 (find the missing element) each have a short student worksheet plus a teacher anecdotal tracker.
- **Summative:** Lesson 5 'Lead Your Own Parade' performance task with a 4-level rubric across A7.1–A7.4, plus a Junior Pattern Conductor certificate.

### Pedagogical notes

- Kindergarten is play-based and oral. Every lesson opens with a circle-time movement game, not a worksheet.
- Worksheets are end-of-lesson consolidation, never the main activity.
- Always introduce a new term orally (e.g. 'core') before showing the printed word.
- Honour student-generated patterns — if a child makes a different valid core, treat it as success.
- Use observational notes in Ontario K Communication-of-Learning style: strengths and growth, not numeric scores.
- Build in opportunities for students to make patterns with their bodies (movement) and voices (sound), not only with paper.

### Canadian context notes

- All recurring characters are Canadian animals (bear, beaver, fox, moose).
- Parade can reference familiar Canadian community parades (Santa Claus parade, Caribbean Carnival, local Canada Day parade) in oral examples without prescribing one.
- Avoid US-only references. Use 'colour' not 'color' in printed materials.

## Lesson 1: Welcome to the Parade!

| Field | Value |

**Big idea:** A pattern is something that repeats in a special order.

**Learning intention (teacher):** We are learning to spot a repeating pattern and predict what comes next.

**Student learning goal:** I can spot a pattern and say what comes next.

**Success criteria:**
- I can show or say what part of a pattern repeats.
- I can predict what comes next in a parade.
- I can build my own AB pattern with my partner.

**Vocabulary introduced:** pattern
**Vocabulary reinforced:** —
**Manipulatives used:** M1_animal_cards, M2_parade_strip

**Lesson-specific materials:**
- Printable Coco the Conductor puppet on a popsicle stick (1 for teacher; template included with manipulatives stage)
- Class anchor chart paper labelled 'What is a pattern?'
- Class diagnostic checklist (provided in assessment_suite stage)
- Optional: small drum or shaker for clap-stomp warm-up

### Minds On (10 min) — Coco's Clap-Stomp Surprise

**Hook:** Today our classroom is hosting a parade — and we are the only ones who can help Coco the Conductor keep his animals in order!

**Teacher script:**

> Bring children to the carpet in a circle. Hold up the Coco puppet.
> 
> Say to students: 'Friends, this is Coco the Conductor. Coco leads parades. A parade is a line of animals or people moving together to a beat. Coco has a problem today — his animals keep getting mixed up. We are going to help him put them in the right order. But first, let's warm up our parade ears.'
> 
> Clap-stomp warm-up: 'Watch and listen.' Clap-stomp, clap-stomp, clap-stomp. Pause. 'What do you think comes next?' Take 2–3 predictions. Repeat 'clap-stomp' once more, then say: 'Yes! When something keeps coming back the same way, again and again, we call that a PATTERN. Say it with me — pattern.' (Class repeats.)
> 
> Write the word PATTERN at the top of the anchor chart with a simple drawing of a clap-and-stomp icon. 'A pattern is something that repeats in a special order. Let's go meet Coco's parade!'

**Student actions:**
- Sit in a circle on the carpet.
- Watch the teacher and predict what comes next in the clap-stomp sequence.
- Repeat the word 'pattern' aloud as a class.

### Action (20 min) — Build the Parade

*Structure:* `whole_group_then_partner`

**Step 1.** Whole group: lay one Parade Strip (M2) flat in front of the carpet. Place Bea, Finn, Bea, Finn on the first four cells. Leave the fifth cell empty.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Coco's parade today goes Bea, Finn, Bea, Finn… who do you think is next?' Take predictions, then place Bea on cell five. 'Yes! That's our pattern: Bea, Finn, Bea, Finn, Bea. Bea and Finn keep coming back, in the same order. That is the part that repeats.'

**Step 2.** Show a second example with three animals: Bea, Finn, Bibi, Bea, Finn, Bibi, ?. Invite a child up to place the next card.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Now Coco's parade is bigger. Bea, Finn, Bibi, Bea, Finn, Bibi… can someone come up and show us who is next?' (After the placement.) 'How did you know? What part keeps repeating?'

**Step 3.** Send children to partner spots. Each pair gets one set of Animal Cards (M1) and one Parade Strip (M2). Their job: build a parade pattern using two animals, repeat it across the strip, and leave one cell empty for their partner to predict.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'You and your partner are going to be parade builders. Pick two animals, build a pattern with them on your strip — but leave one space empty at the end. When you are ready, tap your partner on the shoulder and say: "What comes next?" Your partner has to be a parade detective and figure it out.'

**Step 4.** Circulate. Use the diagnostic checklist to observe which children can extend a pattern, which can describe what repeats, and which can create their own.

> *Teacher prompt:* Stop at each pair. Ask: 'Can you read your parade to me?' Then: 'What part keeps repeating?' Note responses on the checklist.

#### Differentiation

- **For emerging learners:** Pre-fill the first three cells of the parade strip and ask the child only to predict the fourth and fifth. Use only Bea and Finn (high-contrast colours: red and blue vests). Pair with a confident partner.
- **For extending learners:** Challenge the pair to build a three-animal core (e.g., Bea-Finn-Bibi-Bea-Finn-Bibi). Then ask them to swap one animal and predict how the parade changes.
- **For ELL / IEP:** Use the animal name + colour together when speaking ('Bea, the red-vest bear'). Allow the child to point or place a card instead of speaking. Accept gestures or sounds as valid responses.
- **For movement learners:** After building on the strip, the pair stands up and walks the parade — one child calls out animals, the other steps in time.

#### Formative check

- **What to look for:** Can the child predict the next element? Can they describe what is repeating, even informally ('the bear comes back')? Can they build their own pattern with prompting? Without prompting?
- **How to record:** Tick the appropriate column on the diagnostic checklist for each child as you circulate.
- **Tracker columns:** Student name, Predicts next element, Describes what repeats, Creates AB pattern (with prompt), Creates pattern (independent), Notes

### Consolidation (10 min) — Parade Share Circle

**Discussion prompts:**
- Who would like to share a parade you and your partner built?
- What part of your parade keeps coming back?
- Does anyone see a pattern somewhere else in our classroom right now? (e.g. floor tiles, clothing stripes, calendar numbers)

**Exit routine:** As children leave the carpet, each child names one place they will look for a pattern at home tonight.

### Assessment (diagnostic)

- **Evidence collected:** Class checklist completed during the partner-build phase. Captures baseline understanding of A7.1 before Lesson 2 introduces the 'core' concept.
- **Tracker:** `diagnostic_tracker (assembled in assessment_suite stage)`
- **Rationale:** This is the unit's diagnostic moment. The data tells the teacher who already extends a 2-element repeating pattern unprompted, so they can group flexibly for Lesson 2 onward.

### Student worksheet brief — Coco's First Parade

*Independent practice of A7.1 — predicting the next element and creating an AB pattern.*

| # | Task | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Show a parade strip with Bea, Finn, Bea, Finn, ?. Student colours or stickers in the next animal. | draw_or_sticker |
| 2 | Show a longer parade strip with two missing cells in the middle and end. Student fills in both based on the AB pattern shown. | draw_or_sticker |
| 3 | Empty parade strip. Student creates their own AB pattern using two of the four parade animals (drawing or stickers). | create |

**Expected image assets:**
- Bea, Finn, Bibi, Moss thumbnail icons (for student to glue or for printed stickers)
- Parade strip layout matching the M2 manipulative

## Lesson 2: The Beat of the Parade

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Grade | Kindergarten |
| Strand | A. Patterns and Relationships |
| Duration | 40 minutes |
| Primary expectations | A7.2 |
| Secondary expectations | A7.1 |

**Big idea:** Every pattern has a core — the part that repeats.

**Learning intention (teacher):** We are learning to find the core of a repeating pattern and explain why the core matters.

**Student learning goal:** I can find the part of the pattern that repeats. We call it the core.

**Success criteria:**
- I can point to the core of a pattern.
- I can use the loop frame to mark the core.
- I can tell a friend why the core is important.

**Vocabulary introduced:** core
**Vocabulary reinforced:** pattern
**Manipulatives used:** M1_animal_cards, M2_parade_strip, M5_core_loop

**Lesson-specific materials:**
- Drum or shaker (recommended for this lesson)
- Anchor chart from Lesson 1 — add the word CORE today
- Core Loop Frames (M5) — 1 per pair
- Pre-built parade strips with 4 different patterns laid out (teacher prep, ~5 min)
- Class formative tracker (provided in assessment_suite stage)

### Minds On (10 min) — The Drum Discovery

**Hook:** Coco's parade has a beat — and once you hear the beat, the parade can go on forever.

**Teacher script:**

> Gather children on the carpet. Hold up the Coco puppet and the drum.
> 
> Say to students: 'Coco brought a drum today. Listen closely — Coco is going to play the beat of the parade.' Tap the drum while saying animal names: 'Bea — Finn — Bea — Finn — Bea — Finn.' Stop. 'Did you hear something that kept coming back?' Take 2–3 responses. Confirm: 'Yes — Bea-Finn, Bea-Finn, Bea-Finn. That little part that keeps coming back is called the CORE of the pattern.'
> 
> Write CORE on the anchor chart underneath PATTERN. Draw a small loop around an imaginary pair to represent the idea visually. 'The core is the smallest part of the pattern that keeps repeating. Today we are going to be Core Detectives.'

**Student actions:**
- Listen quietly while the teacher drums and chants.
- Identify aloud what they heard repeating.
- Repeat the word 'core' as a class.

### Action (20 min) — Lasso the Core

*Structure:* `whole_group_then_partner`

**Step 1.** Whole group: lay a Parade Strip (M2) flat. Place Bea-Finn-Bea-Finn-Bea-Finn on the six cells. Pick up a Core Loop Frame (M5) and place it around just the first Bea and Finn.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Watch what I do with the loop.' Place the loop around the first Bea-Finn pair. 'I lassoed the core. Bea-Finn is the smallest part that keeps repeating. After Bea-Finn comes Bea-Finn, and after that — Bea-Finn again.'

**Step 2.** Show an ABC pattern: Bea-Finn-Bibi-Bea-Finn-Bibi. Invite a child to come up and place the loop on the core.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Now Coco's parade is bigger. Who can come up and lasso the core?' (After placement.) 'How did you know to put the loop around three cells and not two? Tell us what you noticed.'

**Step 3.** Trick example: lay out a strip with random animals (no repeating order). Ask the class to find the core.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Hmm — where is the core in this one?' Pause for confusion. 'Wait — is there a core? Why not?' Lead to: 'There is no core because nothing is repeating. Without a core, it is not a pattern, just a line of animals.'

**Step 4.** Send children to partner spots. Each pair gets 4 pre-built parade strips and one Core Loop Frame. Their job: place the loop on the core of each strip. Then: build one new parade together and loop its core.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'You and your partner are Core Detectives. You have four parades to solve. Place the loop around the core of each one. When you finish all four, build your own parade and loop your own core. Whisper your core to your partner before you make your loop.'

**Step 5.** Circulate. Use the formative tracker to record which children can identify, loop, and explain the core.

> *Teacher prompt:* Stop at each pair. Ask: 'Where is the core in this parade? How do you know?' Record on the tracker.

#### Differentiation

- **For emerging learners:** Use only AB strips for these children. Pre-mark the loop position with a faint pencil outline so they can place the loop on top.
- **For extending learners:** Hand the pair a strip where the same core appears in two different colourings (e.g., one strip Bea-Finn-Bea-Finn, another Bibi-Moss-Bibi-Moss). Ask: 'Are the cores the same or different? Why?' (Foreshadows Lesson 3.)
- **For ELL / IEP:** Pair physical demonstration with each prompt. Allow the child to nod or place the loop without speaking. Use the words 'this part' and gesture if 'core' is not yet anchored.
- **For movement learners:** After looping the strip, the pair walks the parade. Each time they reach the start of a new core, they say 'CORE!' aloud.

#### Formative check

- **What to look for:** Can the child place the loop on the correct cells? Can they explain that the core is what repeats, in their own words? Do they recognize when there is no core?
- **How to record:** Tick the appropriate column on the formative tracker for each child as you circulate.
- **Tracker columns:** Student name, Identifies core in given pattern, Loops correct number of cells, Explains why it is the core, Recognizes a non-pattern, Notes

### Consolidation (10 min) — Why does the core matter?

**Discussion prompts:**
- Why do we look for the core of a pattern?
- If you know the core, what can you do next?
- What's the difference between a parade with a core and just a line of animals?

**Exit routine:** Quick 'core or no core' sort: teacher claps three short sequences (one with a core, one without, one with). Children show thumbs up for core, thumbs down for no core. Then each child taps the core on a classmate's parade strip before tidying.

### Assessment (formative)

- **Evidence collected:** Class formative tracker for A7.2, plus completed student worksheet 'Find the Beat'.
- **Tracker:** `formative_tracker_lesson_02 (assembled in assessment_suite stage)`
- **Rationale:** A7.2 is the keystone expectation of the unit. The teacher needs to know which children have internalized 'the core is what repeats' before Lesson 3 asks them to translate one core into multiple representations.

*Independent practice of A7.2 — identifying and marking the core, and recognizing when a sequence is or is not a pattern.*

| # | Task | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Three short parade strips, each showing a repeating pattern (one AB, one ABC, one ABB). Student draws a loop or circle around the core of each. | circle_or_loop |
| 2 | A parade strip with the core already boxed at the start. Student extends the pattern by drawing or stickering three more cells. | draw_or_sticker |
| 3 | Empty parade strip. Student creates their own pattern using two or three animals AND draws a loop around their core. | create_and_loop |

**Expected image assets:**
- Bea, Finn, Bibi, Moss thumbnail icons
- Parade strip layout matching M2 manipulative
- An example loop frame illustration in the worksheet header (matching M5 manipulative)

## Lesson 3: Same Beat, Different Parade

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Grade | Kindergarten |
| Strand | A. Patterns and Relationships |
| Duration | 40 minutes |
| Primary expectations | A7.3 |
| Secondary expectations | A7.2 |

**Big idea:** The same core can show up in many ways: colours, sounds, animals, movements.

**Learning intention (teacher):** We are learning that the same core can be shown in many different ways and still be the same pattern.

**Student learning goal:** I can show one pattern in three different ways.

**Success criteria:**
- I can take an animal pattern and turn it into a sound pattern.
- I can take a sound pattern and turn it into a body-movement pattern.
- I can tell my partner what stayed the same and what changed.

**Vocabulary introduced:** —
**Vocabulary reinforced:** pattern, core
**Manipulatives used:** M1_animal_cards, M2_parade_strip, M3_sound_movement_cards, M5_core_loop

**Lesson-specific materials:**
- Anchor chart from previous lessons (no new word needed today; reinforce CORE)
- Three pre-built parade strips for the opening reveal: one animal AB, one sound AB (clap/stomp), one body AB (tall/small)
- Sound and Movement Cards (M3) — full set on the carpet
- Class formative tracker (provided in assessment_suite stage)

### Minds On (10 min) — Coco's Three Parade Surprise

**Hook:** Coco has shown me three different parades today, and I think they are secretly the same parade.

**Teacher script:**

> Bring children to the carpet. Lay all three pre-built strips in a line on the floor: animals, clap/stomp, tall/small.
> 
> Say to students: 'Friends, Coco has a puzzle for us. Coco showed me three parades today. They look very different — one is animals, one is sounds, and one is our bodies. But Coco told me a secret: the THREE parades are really the SAME parade.' Pause. 'Do you believe that?' Take responses. (Most will say no.)
> 
> 'Today we are going to find out how three parades can be the same. We have to listen for the beat — the part that repeats. The CORE.' Touch the anchor chart at the word CORE. 'If the core is the same, the pattern is the same — even if the things look or sound different.'

**Student actions:**
- Sit in a circle on the carpet, facing the three parade strips.
- Predict whether the three parades are the same or different.
- Listen and watch as the teacher leads the reveal.

### Action (22 min) — Translate the Parade

*Structure:* `whole_group_then_partner`

**Step 1.** Whole group: see the expanded walkthrough below. The opening reveal of the three same-core parades is fully scripted there.

> *Teacher prompt:* Follow the expanded_walkthrough section verbatim. After the reveal moment, leave all three strips and their loops in view as a reference for partner work.

**Step 2.** Hand each pair a pre-built animal strip showing one core (assign by readiness — AB, ABB, or ABC). Their job is to translate that same core into a sound version using Sound and Movement Cards (M3), and into a body-movement version (no cards — they invent it).

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'You and your partner just got an animal parade. Your job is to make TWO more parades with the same core. One with sound cards. One with your bodies. The core has to stay the same — only the way you show it changes. When you are ready, tap your partner and parade together.'

**Step 3.** Circulate. Each pair must show all three versions before they finish. Ask: 'What stayed the same in all three?' and 'What changed?'

> *Teacher prompt:* Stop at each pair. Ask a child to read all three versions: animal, sound, body. Then: 'What stayed the same?' (the core / the beat) 'What changed?' (the animals / sounds / bodies). Record on the formative tracker.

**Step 4.** Optional extension: pairs swap the core they were given for a different one and translate it into all three forms.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to extending pairs: 'You finished — now Coco wants to test you. Here is a NEW core. Same job. Three parades.'

#### Differentiation

- **For emerging learners:** Stay with AB cores only. Pre-place the first cell of the sound version and the first body position to give a launchpad. Pair with a confident peer who can model the body version first.
- **For extending learners:** Use ABB or ABC cores. After the three translations, ask: 'Can you show the SAME core a fourth way? Could it be a colour pattern? A high/low voice pattern?'
- **For ELL / IEP:** Lean on the body-movement translation as the entry point — it requires no language. Once the child can do tall-small or jump-freeze, scaffold backward to the cards. Accept gestures as proof of understanding.
- **For movement learners:** These children often grasp this lesson fastest. Invite them to lead the class through their body version during consolidation.

#### Formative check

- **What to look for:** Can the child build a second and third version of the same core? Can they articulate, in any words, that the core stayed the same and only the surface changed? Do they self-correct when their second version drifts off the core?
- **How to record:** Tick the appropriate column on the formative tracker.
- **Tracker columns:** Student name, Translates to sound version, Translates to body version, Names what stayed the same, Names what changed, Notes

### Consolidation (8 min) — Three-Parade Showcase

**Discussion prompts:**
- Who would like to show their three parades to the class? (Take 2–3 pairs.)
- When you watched [pair name]'s three parades, what stayed the same?
- Could we make this same core a FOURTH way? What else could we use?

**Exit routine:** Each child whispers to a partner: 'My core was ___, and I showed it three ways: with ___, ___, and ___.' Then tidy.

### Assessment (formative)

- **Evidence collected:** Class formative tracker for A7.3, plus completed student worksheet 'Same Beat, Three Ways'.
- **Tracker:** `formative_tracker_lesson_03 (assembled in assessment_suite stage)`
- **Rationale:** A7.3 is the most abstract of the four expectations because it requires children to look past surface features to the underlying core. The teacher needs evidence of who has crossed that abstraction line before Lesson 4 (extending and missing elements) requires it implicitly.

### Expanded walkthrough — The Three Parades Reveal

*Why this lesson warrants extra scripting:* Children at K naturally read patterns by surface features (the animals, the colours). A7.3 requires them to abstract past the surface to the underlying core. This is the unit's conceptual hinge, and the moment is fragile — many children will say 'no, they are different' the first time. This script walks the teacher through the reveal step by step, including how to recover when the abstraction does not land.

**Setup:** Three short parade strips, all sharing the SAME AB core, laid side by side on the carpet: (1) Bea-Finn-Bea-Finn animal version, (2) clap-stomp-clap-stomp version using Sound and Movement Cards, (3) tall-small-tall-small using body-position cards or photos. A Core Loop Frame ready in the teacher's hand. Coco the puppet visible. Anchor chart with the word CORE in view.

**Teacher script:**

> Hold up Coco. Say to students: 'Coco has shown me three different parades today, and Coco says they are secretly the SAME parade. Let's see if you can figure out why.'
> 
> Point to Strip 1 (animals). 'Read this parade with me.' Lead the class: 'Bea, Finn, Bea, Finn.' Place the loop around the first Bea-Finn pair. 'And what is the core here?' (Bea-Finn.)
> 
> Point to Strip 2 (clap-stomp). 'Now this parade — read it with your hands and feet.' Lead the class: clap, stomp, clap, stomp. Pick up the loop and place it around the first clap-stomp pair. 'What is the core of this one?' (clap-stomp.)
> 
> Point to Strip 3 (tall-small). 'And this one — let's do it with our bodies. Stand TALL when I point here, crouch SMALL when I point there.' Lead the class through: tall, small, tall, small. Loop the first tall-small pair. 'What is the core?' (tall-small.)
> 
> Pause. Step back. 'I told you Coco said these three parades were secretly the same. Are they?' (Most children will still say no.)
> 
> 'Hmm — let me put the loops next to each other.' Slide the three strips so the loops align in a column. 'Look at every loop. In every loop, there is a FIRST thing and a SECOND thing. Then those two things come back. Bea-Finn, Bea-Finn. Clap-stomp, clap-stomp. Tall-small, tall-small. The CORE is the same. Only the way we are showing it is different.'
> 
> Pause and let it land. 'When the core is the same, the pattern is the SAME pattern. Coco doesn't care if it's bears, claps, or bodies. Coco cares about the BEAT.'

**Expected student responses:**
- Several children will insist the parades are different because the things in them are different — this is normal and expected.
- Some children will count cells ('they all have four') without seeing AB structure.
- A child may say 'they all go 1-2, 1-2' before the teacher names it — validate this.
- A child may name the AB structure in their own words ('one of these, one of those, again').

**Common misconceptions:**
- 'They are different patterns because one has bears and the other has claps.' — surface-feature thinking, the most common K response.
- 'It's the same because they have the same number of things.' — confusion between length and core.
- 'It's the same because they all have two animals.' — partial insight; the child sees structure but lacks vocabulary.

**Recovery moves:**
- If a child is stuck on surface features: physically slide all three loops on top of each other and ask 'do the loops have two things in each? Do the two things come back in the same order?'
- If a child confuses length and core: lay out an 8-cell animal parade and a 4-cell clap-stomp parade with the same AB core. Ask 'is the core the same?' Lead them to see length is independent of core.
- If a child names the structure in their own words: validate first, then supply the formal language. 'Yes — you said one of these, one of those, again. Mathematicians call that an AB core.'

## Lesson 4: Fix the Broken Parade

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Grade | Kindergarten |
| Strand | A. Patterns and Relationships |
| Duration | 40 minutes |
| Primary expectations | A7.4 |
| Secondary expectations | A7.1, A7.2 |

**Big idea:** We can extend a pattern forward and backward, and we can fill in missing pieces.

**Learning intention (teacher):** We are learning to find what is missing in a pattern and to extend a pattern in both directions.

**Student learning goal:** I can find what is missing in a pattern and add what comes next.

**Success criteria:**
- I can find a missing animal in the middle of a parade.
- I can extend a parade to make it longer at the end.
- I can extend a parade backward to show what came before the start.

**Vocabulary introduced:** missing, extend
**Vocabulary reinforced:** pattern, core
**Manipulatives used:** M1_animal_cards, M2_parade_strip, M4_missing_card, M5_core_loop

**Lesson-specific materials:**
- Anchor chart from previous lessons — add MISSING and EXTEND today
- Missing Mystery Cards (M4) — 5 per pair
- Pre-built parade strips for the Fix-It and Stretch-It stations (teacher prep, ~10 min)
- Class formative tracker (provided in assessment_suite stage)

### Minds On (10 min) — Coco's Big Trouble

**Hook:** There was a windstorm last night, and now Coco's parade has GAPS! We need to figure out who's missing.

**Teacher script:**

> Bring children to the carpet. Hold up Coco. Have a parade strip pre-laid with a Missing Mystery Card in the middle: Bea — Finn — [M4 card] — Finn — Bea — Finn.
> 
> Say to students: 'Friends, Coco needs our help. There was a windstorm last night, and look what happened to Coco's parade.' Point to the M4 card. 'See this question-mark card? It means an animal is MISSING. We don't know who used to be there. Can we figure it out?'
> 
> Let children study it. Take 2–3 predictions. Lead them to the answer (Bea). 'How did you know? What part of the pattern told you?' (The core — Bea-Finn keeps coming back.) Add MISSING to the anchor chart with a small picture of a question mark.
> 
> 'And there is one more new word for today.' Hold up two cards: Bea, Finn. 'If I want this parade to keep going, I have to EXTEND it. EXTEND means to make the parade longer.' Add EXTEND to the anchor chart. 'Today we have two jobs: FIX missing animals, and EXTEND parades to make them longer — at the end AND at the start.'

**Student actions:**
- Sit on the carpet and study the parade with the missing card.
- Predict who the missing animal is.
- Repeat the words 'missing' and 'extend' as a class.

### Action (22 min) — Fix-It and Stretch-It

*Structure:* `small_group`

**Step 1.** Phase 1 — Fix-It (10 min). Each pair gets 3 pre-built parade strips, each with one Missing Mystery Card placed somewhere (middle, start, or end). Pair must remove the M4 card and replace it with the correct animal card.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Phase one is FIX-IT. You have three broken parades. Find the missing animal in each one and fix it. Whisper your answer to your partner before you swap the card. When all three are fixed, raise your hand.'

**Step 2.** Phase 2 — Stretch-It (10 min). Each pair receives a parade strip with 4 cells already filled in the MIDDLE of the strip, leaving 2 empty cells at the END and 2 empty cells at the START. They must extend the parade in both directions.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Phase two is STRETCH-IT. Look at the four animals in the middle. Now we have to make this parade longer. Two animals come AFTER the end. And — this is the tricky part — two animals come BEFORE the start. What animal would be standing at the very front of the line, before the parade you can see?'

If needed, model with a child: 'If the parade goes Bea-Finn-Bea-Finn, what would have come before the first Bea? It would have to be Finn — because the pattern goes back the same way it goes forward.'

**Step 3.** Circulate between phases. Use the formative tracker to record which children can fill missing in the middle, missing at the end (easier), missing at the start (harder), extend forward, and extend backward (hardest).

> *Teacher prompt:* Stop at each pair. Ask: 'How did you know that animal was missing?' (Phase 1.) 'How did you know what came BEFORE the start?' (Phase 2.) Record on the tracker.

#### Differentiation

- **For emerging learners:** Phase 1 only. Use AB patterns and place the M4 card always at the end (predicting next, like Lesson 1). Skip backward-extension; if time, do forward-extension only.
- **For extending learners:** Use ABC or ABBA cores. In Phase 1, give two M4 cards in the same strip. In Phase 2, ask the pair to extend three cells in each direction and explain how they decided.
- **For ELL / IEP:** Walk the parade physically. Place the strip on the floor and have the child step on each cell while saying the animal aloud. When you reach an M4 card, the child stops and the partner names what should be there. For backward extension, walk the parade in reverse.
- **For movement learners:** Replace cards with floor spots. Walk the parade forward to predict the end; walk it backward to predict the start. Body memory helps the abstraction land.

#### Formative check

- **What to look for:** Can the child identify a missing element in the middle? At the end? At the start (harder — many K children find this hard)? Can they extend the parade forward? Backward? Do they reference the core when explaining their answer?
- **How to record:** Tick the appropriate column on the formative tracker.
- **Tracker columns:** Student name, Finds missing in middle, Finds missing at end, Finds missing at start, Extends forward, Extends backward, Notes

### Consolidation (8 min) — Two-Direction Parade Share

**Discussion prompts:**
- Who fixed a tricky missing animal? Show us where it was.
- Who extended their parade BACKWARD? How did you figure out what came before the start?
- Why is finding the core important when something is missing?

**Exit routine:** Each child whispers to a partner: 'I fixed a parade where ___ was missing.' OR 'I extended my parade by adding ___ at the start.' Then tidy.

### Assessment (formative)

- **Evidence collected:** Class formative tracker for A7.4, plus completed student worksheet 'Help Coco Fix It!'.
- **Tracker:** `formative_tracker_lesson_04 (assembled in assessment_suite stage)`
- **Rationale:** A7.4 has two parts (extend in both directions; identify missing). Backward extension is the hardest concept for K children because they typically read patterns left-to-right. The tracker separates these so the teacher can see which children are ready for the summative and which need more bridging.

## Lesson 5: Lead Your Own Parade

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Grade | Kindergarten |
| Strand | A. Patterns and Relationships |
| Duration | 40 minutes |
| Primary expectations | A7.1, A7.2, A7.3, A7.4 |
| Secondary expectations | — |

**Big idea:** Pattern-makers plan a core, repeat it, extend it, and explain it.

**Learning intention (teacher):** We are learning to plan, build, lead, and explain a parade pattern that we made ourselves.

**Student learning goal:** I can plan a parade pattern, lead it, and explain the core to a friend.

**Success criteria:**
- I can plan a parade with a core that repeats.
- I can build my parade and extend it forward and backward.
- I can show my core a second way using sounds or my body.
- I can explain my core to my partner.

**Vocabulary introduced:** —
**Vocabulary reinforced:** pattern, core, missing, extend
**Manipulatives used:** M1_animal_cards, M2_parade_strip, M3_sound_movement_cards, M5_core_loop

**Lesson-specific materials:**
- Anchor chart from previous four lessons (PATTERN, CORE, MISSING, EXTEND visible)
- Junior Pattern Conductor certificates (provided in assessment_suite stage; one per student)
- Summative rubric — one copy on a clipboard per teacher (provided in assessment_suite stage)
- Optional: small handheld bell or shaker for the closing celebration

### Minds On (10 min) — The Pattern Conductor's Toolbox

**Hook:** Today is the big day. You have everything you need in your toolbox — and YOU are the conductor.

**Teacher script:**

> Bring children to the carpet. Stand by the anchor chart, which now has all four words: PATTERN, CORE, MISSING, EXTEND. Hold up Coco.
> 
> Say to students: 'Friends, look at our chart. We have learned so much. Day 1 we learned what a PATTERN is. Day 2 we found the CORE. Day 3 we showed the same core in three different ways. Day 4 we fixed parades that were MISSING animals, and we learned to EXTEND them — forward AND backward.' Tap each word as you say it.
> 
> 'Today is your big day. Today YOU are the Pattern Conductor. You will plan a parade. You will build it. You will show it a second way. And you will lead a friend through it. Coco is going to be watching very carefully — and so am I — because today everyone gets to earn the Junior Pattern Conductor certificate.'
> 
> Quick whole-class warm-up: clap-stomp three times. 'Ready, conductors? Let's begin.'

**Student actions:**
- Sit on the carpet and review the four anchor-chart words as a class.
- Repeat the four words aloud as the teacher taps each.
- Move to their desk or workspace when dismissed.

### Action (25 min) — Plan, Build, Translate, Lead

*Structure:* `individual`

**Step 1.** Phase 1 — Plan (5 min). Each child receives the 'Lead Your Own Parade' worksheet (page 1). They draw or sticker their chosen core in the 'My Core' box and place a loop around it. They may pick AB, ABB, ABC, or ABBA — their choice.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'First job: plan your CORE. Pick how many things will be in your core. Draw it in the My Core box. Then circle it with the loop — that shows you know what repeats.'

**Step 2.** Phase 2 — Build (10 min). Children build their parade on the worksheet's parade strip, extending the core forward AND backward to fill 8 cells. Teacher circulates with the summative rubric clipboard and begins one-on-one conferences during this phase.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Now build your parade. The middle four animals are easy — they come right from your core, repeating it. The hard part is the two on each end. Make sure they keep the pattern going BOTH ways. I will come around and ask each of you to read your parade to me.'

**Step 3.** Phase 3 — Translate (5 min). Children fill the second strip on their worksheet with the SAME core, but using sounds OR body icons (their choice). They can reference the M3 cards if needed. Teacher continues conferences.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Last building job: show your same core a second way. Sounds, like clap-stomp. Or bodies, like tall-small. Same core — just a different way of showing it.'

**Step 4.** Phase 4 — Lead (5 min). Children move to the carpet in pairs. Each child leads their partner through their parade: reads the animal version aloud, points to and explains the core, walks the partner through the second version, and finally asks the partner 'What would come after my last animal? What would come before my first animal?' Then they swap.

> *Teacher prompt:* Say to students: 'Final job. Find a partner. Take turns being the conductor. When it's your turn: read your parade out loud, show your partner your core, lead them through your second version, and ask them what comes next AND what came before. Then it's your partner's turn.'

#### Differentiation

- **For emerging learners:** AB cores only. Pre-print the parade strip with the first cell of the core already filled to give a launchpad. Allow the child to lead a single partner with the teacher beside them, scaffolding the four-step lead routine.
- **For extending learners:** Encourage ABBA, AABB, or ABC cores. After their partner-lead, invite them to lead a small group of 3–4 children through their parade. Ask them to explain WHY their second version has the same core.
- **For ELL / IEP:** Allow the child to lead by walking the parade physically rather than explaining verbally. Accept gestures and pointing as evidence. The teacher conference can be visual: 'Show me your core. Show me what comes next. Show me your second way.'
- **For movement learners:** These children typically excel at the Lead phase. Encourage them to invent a body-movement version and lead the partner physically through it — turning the worksheet into performance.

#### Formative check

- **What to look for:** This is the summative — record evidence per expectation. A7.1: did the child create a valid pattern? A7.2: can they identify and explain the core? A7.3: did they produce a second representation with the same core? A7.4: did they extend in both directions and answer 'what comes before' and 'what comes after' correctly?
- **How to record:** Use the summative rubric (one row per child, one column per expectation, score 1–4). Record evidence-based anecdotal notes for any score below 3 or any standout score of 4.
- **Tracker columns:** Student name, A7.1 level (1-4), A7.2 level (1-4), A7.3 level (1-4), A7.4 level (1-4), Anecdotal evidence

### Consolidation (5 min) — Junior Pattern Conductor Ceremony

**Discussion prompts:**
- What was your favourite part of being the Pattern Conductor today?
- What was the trickiest part?
- Who would you like to thank for helping you learn about patterns this week?

**Exit routine:** Gather on the carpet. Each child receives a Junior Pattern Conductor certificate (printed from the assessment_suite stage). The teacher reads each child's name aloud, says one specific thing they observed during the summative ('I saw _____ extend a parade backward all by themselves'), and hands them the certificate. Class applauds after each. Optional: ring a small bell or shaker after each name.

### Assessment (summative)

- **Evidence collected:** Completed 'Lead Your Own Parade' worksheet (Phases 1–3 visible artifact), one-on-one teacher conference notes captured during Phase 2/3, and partner-lead observation from Phase 4. Each child receives a 1–4 score on the summative rubric for each of A7.1, A7.2, A7.3, A7.4.
- **Tracker:** `summative_rubric (assembled in assessment_suite stage)`
- **Rationale:** This is the unit's summative. The four-phase structure (Plan-Build-Translate-Lead) is deliberately designed so that each phase generates evidence for at least one expectation. Plan/Build covers A7.1 and A7.2; Translate covers A7.3; Lead covers A7.4 (and re-anchors A7.2). The teacher can score most children during conferences in Phase 2/3 and confirm during the Lead phase.

### Overall prep notes

Total prep time across all 7 assets: approximately 90 minutes for a class of 20. Default print spec is BLACK AND WHITE for every asset — this ensures any printer in any classroom can produce the unit. Colour printing is suggested in several assets (M1, M3, Coco) for visual richness only; the unit works equally well in B&W because animal vests use distinct pattern fills (solid / stripes / dots / checkered) and Coco is line art that children can colour as a Day-0 activity. Recommended prep approach: prep the entire unit's manipulatives in one sitting before Lesson 1 — front-loads the work but means the unit runs smoothly day-to-day. Alternative: prep M1 + M2 + M6 + Coco before Lesson 1 (~55 min), then prep M5 the night before Lesson 2, M3 before Lesson 3, and M4 before Lesson 4. Storage: keep all sets in a labelled bin or shelf, with each pair's set in its own bag. The animal cards (M1) are by far the most-used asset — laminate these even if you skip lamination on others.

### Pattern Parade — Summative Rubric

*Used during and after Lesson 5 (Lead Your Own Parade) to score each child against all four expectations on a 4-level scale. Aligned with Ontario Kindergarten Communication of Learning conventions: descriptive, observational, growth-oriented.*

| Expectation | Level 1 (Beginning) | Level 2 (Developing) | Level 3 (Achieving — goal) | Level 4 (Exceeding) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A7.1** — identify, describe, extend, and create patterns | With significant adult prompting, names what comes next in a 2-element repeating pattern only some of the time. Does not yet create patterns independently. | Predicts what comes next in a 2-element pattern most of the time. Creates an AB pattern with teacher prompts and a partner. | Confidently predicts and describes a repeating pattern. Independently creates an AB pattern using two animals and explains the order. | Spontaneously creates patterns with 3-element cores or longer and applies them in play. Notices patterns in the classroom environment without being asked. |
| **A7.2** — identify the core of a repeating pattern and describe why it is important | Cannot consistently isolate the core. May circle the entire strip or just one cell when asked to find the core. | Identifies the core in simple AB patterns with adult support. Struggles with ABB or ABC cores. | Independently identifies the core in AB, ABB, and ABC patterns. Explains that the core is 'the part that repeats.' | Articulates why the core matters (e.g. 'once you know the core, you can keep the parade going forever') and uses the core to predict beyond what is shown. |
| **A7.3** — show that patterns can be represented in different ways because the core remains the same | Treats different representations of the same core as different patterns. Surface-feature thinking dominates ('they are not the same because one has bears'). | With teacher modelling, can produce a second version of a given core, but the structure often drifts (e.g., AB animal turns into ABB sound). | Independently translates one core into 2 different representations (animal / sound / body) while preserving the core. | Translates one core into 3 or more representations and articulates that the structure (the core) is what makes them the same — not the surface elements. |
| **A7.4** — extend patterns in both directions and identify any missing elements | Cannot fill missing elements. May put random animals in gaps. Does not yet extend patterns. | Fills missing elements at the end of a pattern (predicting next) but struggles with middle and start positions. Forward extension only. | Fills missing elements anywhere in a pattern. Extends a pattern forward correctly. May still need prompts for backward extension. | Independently extends patterns in both directions, including reasoning backward, and explains how the core determines what came before the start. |

### Lead Your Own Parade — Summative Administration Guide

*Ties the Lesson 5 worksheet, the lesson plan's four phases (Plan-Build-Translate-Lead), and the rubric together. Tells the teacher exactly what evidence to look for during each phase and which expectation each phase's evidence maps to.*

#### Administration steps

1. Before the lesson: photocopy one summative worksheet ('Lead Your Own Parade') per child. Print one rubric per child or maintain a single class rubric on a clipboard with student-name rows pre-filled. Have the Junior Pattern Conductor certificates printed and ready for the closing ceremony (do not show them to children before they have earned them).
2. Phase 1 (Plan, 5 min): Children draw their core in the My Core box and loop it. This produces visible evidence for A7.2. Walk the room and check that each child has identified a core; nudge children who have drawn random animals.
3. Phase 2 (Build, 10 min): Children fill the 8-cell parade strip extending their core forward and backward. This produces visible evidence for A7.1 (creates a pattern) and A7.4 (extends in both directions). Begin one-on-one rubric conferences during this phase — ask each child to read their parade aloud and answer 'what comes after the last animal? what came before the first animal?'.
4. Phase 3 (Translate, 5 min): Children translate the same core into a sound or body version on the second strip. This produces visible evidence for A7.3. Continue rubric conferences. By the end of Phase 3, you should have scored most children on at least 3 of 4 expectations.
5. Phase 4 (Lead, 5 min): Children pair up on the carpet and lead each other through their parade. Listen for partners articulating the core, predicting next, and predicting before. Use this phase to confirm scores you were uncertain about and to upgrade scores where a child demonstrates Level 4 thinking aloud.
6. After the lesson: review the rubric with the worksheet artifact in front of you. For any child below Level 3 on any expectation, note what evidence (or absence) drove that decision. Use this for the Communication of Learning narrative.

**Scoring guidance:** Use the worksheet as primary evidence and the partner-lead conversation as confirming evidence. For each expectation, ask: 'What level of independence and confidence did this child show?' Level 3 is the grade-level goal — aim for most children to land at L2 or L3. Children who are L4 on every expectation are ready for Grade 1 pattern work; document this for the receiving teacher. Children who are L1 on multiple expectations need targeted small-group work in the next math block — do not retain them in K patterns; instead build the bridge in concrete play and revisit.

#### Evidence per expectation

- **A7.1** — Look at Part 2 of the worksheet (Build the parade). Did the child create a valid repeating pattern across the 8 cells? Combined with Part 1 (the core they invented). A child who fills Part 2 with a coherent repetition of their Part 1 core is at Level 3 on A7.1.
- **A7.2** — Look at Part 1 (My Core box). Did the child identify a core, and is it correctly looped? Then check Part 2 — does the parade actually use that core consistently? Children who looped 1 cell or the entire strip are at L1; children who looped a valid 2-3 cell core are at L3 minimum.
- **A7.3** — Look at Part 3 (Show it another way). Does the second representation use the SAME structural core as Part 2? A child who fills Part 3 with sounds or bodies that follow the same AB / ABB / ABC structure is at Level 3. A child who drifts in structure (AB → ABB) is at Level 2.
- **A7.4** — Look at Part 2 cells 1–2 (backward extension) and 7–8 (forward extension). Combined with the partner-lead conversation in Phase 4 ('what came before? what comes after?'). A child who fills all 8 cells correctly AND can articulate what came before the first animal is at Level 3 or 4.
